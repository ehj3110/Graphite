### 1. Topological & Crystallographic Selection Rules

The transition viability between distinct PAM orientations is governed by the Edge-to-Edge Matching Model, which dictates that stable interfaces require minimizing the misfit between continuous wireframe struts and the projected void repeat distances of the receiving lattice.

A transition is analytically classified as **Topologically Forbidden** if the projected cross-sectional footprint of the entering strut cluster strictly exceeds the effective aperture diameter ratio ($D_{\text{ap}}/a$) of the receiving habit plane. For example, $\{100\}$ habit planes present square faces and foreshortened triangular facets, which fundamentally struggle to accommodate out-of-plane strut insertion, resulting in a 69.4% aperture clash failure rate and permitting only single axial ring piercing. Conversely, $\{111\}$ habit planes present equilateral triangular facets and large hexagonal voids that natively support maximum inscribed clearance, Borromean ring triads, and 3-cycle strut threading.

### 2. Closed-Form Analytical Formulas for Cross-Plane Grain Boundaries

For cross-plane boundaries where habit normals differ (e.g., $\{100\} \leftrightarrow \{110\}$), the gap $g^*$ and shift $\mathbf{s}^*$ must be dynamically evaluated based on the geometric projection of the boundary faces.

**Out-of-Plane Gap ($g^*$):**
The ideal gap aligns the out-of-plane projection of both bounding envelopes such that the entering node reaches the exact depth of the receiving void's barycenter.

$$g^*(a, r_w) = \frac{1}{2} \left( \Delta z_A + \Delta z_B \right) - r_w \left( \frac{1}{\cos(\phi_A)} + \frac{1}{\cos(\phi_B)} \right)$$

where $\Delta z$ is the interplanar depth to the first internal nodal tier of the respective sublattice, and $\phi$ is the insertion angle of the primary struts relative to the habit plane normal.

**In-Plane Translation ($\mathbf{s}^*$):**
Instead of aligning solid atomic sites, PAMs utilize an Inverted Void O-Lattice Alignment, which maximizes clearance by mapping the solid penetrating vertex of Lattice A ($\mathbf{x}^V_A$) directly to the receiving void centroid of Lattice B ($\mathbf{c}_{F_B}$).

$$\mathbf{s}^* = \mathbf{c}_{F_B} - \mathbf{x}^V_A$$

**Balancing Mismatched Periodicities:**
To resolve transverse 2D Bravais lattice mismatches (e.g., square $\{100\}$ vs. rectangular $\{110\}$), you must enforce a Coincidence Supercell matcher that identifies integer multipliers $\mathbf{M}$ and twist angles $\theta$. The exact analytical balance is found by solving:

$$\mathbf{M}_1 \mathbf{A}_1 = \mathbf{R}(\theta) \mathbf{M}_2 \mathbf{A}_2$$

Enforcing this supercell condition guarantees that void barycenters align periodically across the interface, eliminating long-range phase drift and enabling collision-free multi-cell boundaries.

### 3. Coherent Twin Boundaries ($\Sigma 3$ and $\Sigma 9$)

For a $\Sigma 3$ $\{111\}$ coherent twin boundary on a `D4-TET` lattice, the boundary acts as a pure mathematical mirror reflection. Because the lattice boundary is geometrically invariant under this reflection, the void barycenters of the parent perfectly align with the void barycenters of the twin.

The analytical symmetry operations are:

* **Rotation/Reflection Matrix ($\mathbf{R}_{\text{twin}}$):**
For the $\{111\}$ plane with normal $\mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}[1, 1, 1]^T$:

$$\mathbf{R}_{\text{twin}} = \mathbf{I} - 2\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^T = \frac{1}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

* **In-Plane Shift ($\mathbf{t}_{\text{twin}}$):**

$$\mathbf{t}_{\text{twin}} = [0, 0, 0]$$

This zero-misfit transition guarantees simultaneous multi-face catenation and maximizes steric clearance because no translational dislocation is required to bypass aperture bottlenecks.

### 4. Biomimetic Boundary Adapter Cells (Supermesh Homotopy)

To bridge highly incompatible symmetries (e.g., square $\{100\}$ to hexagonal $\{111\}$), a "Supermesh Homotopy" algorithm projects wireframe graphs onto a common unit sphere to generate a single boundary adapter cell.
